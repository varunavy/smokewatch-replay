\subsection{Related Work}

\subsubsection{Kafka and replay}
Kafka was introduced as a distributed messaging system for collecting and
delivering high volumes of log data with low latency~\cite{kreps2011kafka}.
Because consumers pull messages, a consumer can be rewound, which makes a
recorded stream straightforward to replay~\cite{kreps2011kafka}.

\subsubsection{Stream benchmarks}
Chintapalli et al.~\cite{chintapalli2016benchmarking} benchmarked Storm, Flink and
Spark Streaming using a full pipeline built on Kafka and Redis rather than testing
raw event-processing speed, and compared 99th-percentile latency and throughput.
Karimov et al.~\cite{karimov2018benchmarking} proposed a benchmarking framework for
Storm, Spark and Flink that focuses on the throughput and latency of windowed
operations and separates the system under test from the driver. Our replay
producer plays the role of the driver, and our conditions vary replay speed and
delay rather than the engine.

\subsubsection{Late and out-of-order data}
Events can reach a stream processor in a different order from their event times,
for example because of slow sources or network delays. Grulich et
al.~\cite{grulich2019generating} describe a generator that creates reproducible
out-of-order streams from recorded data by delaying selected events by a
configured amount and ordering the stream by the resulting arrival time. SmokeWatch
applies the same basic idea, delaying selected recorded events by a deterministic
rule, to three real environmental sources replayed through Kafka.

\subsubsection{Streaming algorithms: Bloom filters}
Bloom filters~\cite{bloom1970} trade a small false-positive probability for a large
saving in space, and have many variants~\cite{broder2004network}. Double hashing,
where two hash values simulate additional hash functions, can be used without
losing the asymptotic false-positive rate~\cite{kirsch2008less}. A standard filter
eventually fills up as items accumulate; the Stable Bloom
Filter~\cite{deng2006stable} was designed for unbounded streams by evicting stale
information, at the cost of a small false-negative rate.
